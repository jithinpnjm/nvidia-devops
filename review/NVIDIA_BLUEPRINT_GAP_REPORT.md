# NVIDIA Certification Blueprint Gap Report

Audit-only pass against three NVIDIA Professional certification blueprints (NCP-AII, NCP-AIN, NCP-AIOL), scoped to `docs/nvidia-zero-to-hero/` (volumes 01–24). No `docs/` files were edited. Method: grepped the full tree for each named technology/tool (exact strings and case-insensitive variants, not just chapter titles), then read the surrounding chapter content to judge depth, not just presence.

## 1. Executive summary

The curriculum's underlying mechanisms are taught deeply and consistently — InfiniBand, RoCE/PFC/ECN/DCQCN, NVLink/NVSwitch, GPUDirect, MIG/time-slicing, DCGM, GPU Operator, checkpointing, distributed training math — all CORE-level with real evidence, decision trees, and interview framing. The gap is narrower than it looks: it is almost entirely **named-product coverage that sits on top of mechanisms already taught**, not missing mechanism knowledge. Of ~30 blueprint items checked:

- **~14 already covered sufficiently or deeply** — no action needed (IB/RoCE mechanisms, GPU Operator, MIG/time-slicing/vGPU, DCGM, checkpointing, PCIe/NVLink topology, GPUDirect RDMA/Storage, capacity/chargeback concepts, acceptance-testing concept generally).
- **~7 genuinely missing named products/workflows worth real (Level A/B) work**: Slurm mechanics (slurmctld/sbatch/srun) + Enroot + Pyxis in Volume 13, NVIDIA Network Operator in Volume 10, UFM as a named product in Volume 08, BCM operations depth in Volume 19, HPL/ClusterKit cluster burn-in in Volume 17, Mission Control awareness in Volume 19/21, an explicit AI Factory commissioning-sequence section in Volume 21.
- **~9 missing but correctly scoped as Level C/D light-touch**: Run:ai (Volume 11), Magnum IO umbrella (Volume 07/15), NetQ/NVUE/NVIDIA Air/BGP-EVPN (Volume 09), WJH (thin, needs one paragraph, Volume 08/09), Volume 20 cross-reference tags to these named tools.

**Overall read: this is a worthwhile pass, but a small one.** Nearly every fix is a subsection added to an existing chapter (or one new focused chapter in Volume 13), not a new volume. The single largest real gap — Slurm/Enroot/Pyxis workflow mechanics — is also the one the user's own brief already flagged as the most important. Total estimated effort is roughly 3–5 focused writing sessions, not a curriculum rework. Volume 19's chapters 4–12 being stubs is the bigger structural gap in the book overall, and several blueprint items (BCM ops, Slurm ops, Run:ai, Mission Control) naturally belong inside that missing content rather than as separate additions — see §6.

## 5. Is a new "NVIDIA Professional Blueprint Coverage" matrix document worth creating?

**Yes, but as a thin reference artifact, not new content.** Given the number of named-product items now scattered as small subsections across 8+ volumes (per §3), a single matrix (`Blueprint | Objective | Depth | Book Volume | Chapter/Section | Lab | Official NVIDIA Reference`) has real value for interview prep — it lets the user quickly answer "does the book cover X" without hunting. It should **not** live under `docs/nvidia-zero-to-hero/` as a numbered volume (that would violate the anti-scope-creep instruction and implies it's learning content). Recommended location: repo root, alongside the other audit/reference docs already there (`JD_COVERAGE_GAP_REPORT.md`, `CONTENT_COVERAGE_REPORT.md`) — e.g. `NVIDIA_BLUEPRINT_COVERAGE_MATRIX.md`. Build it only after the §3 additions land, so the "Chapter/Section" column points at real content rather than TODOs.
